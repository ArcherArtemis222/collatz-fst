\title{Machine-Checked Expressivity No-Go Theorems for Finite-State
  Additive Ranking Templates in Accelerated Collatz Dynamics}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We present machine-checked expressivity no-go theorems for finite-state
additive ranking templates in accelerated Collatz dynamics. In Lean~4
we formalize a binary carry transducer that implements the accelerated
map exactly; its occupation vectors are path flows on a finite graph,
and a single Kirchhoff conservation law unifies the local counting
relations. The difference-feature spaces have exact dimension $10$
(Level~2) and $31$ (Level~3, two-mode), and machine-checked Farkas
certificates refute strict descent for three template classes, with
universal quantification over odd $x > 1$ and an affine strengthening
with unconstrained per-mode intercepts. All certificates are re-derived
in exact rational arithmetic in continuous integration, and every
theorem stated in the paper is kernel-checked against its Lean
original. This paper does not prove the Collatz conjecture, and it does
not establish the impossibility of all finite-state methods; the scope
boundary is made precise by a three-layer classification of
finite-state potentials.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% =========================================================================

% Contributions (1)-(4) in the outline's fixed order; summary of
% Sections 2-5 only — the introduction adds no new claims (W6 rule).

This paper proves, and machine-checks in Lean~4, a family of
\emph{expressivity no-go theorems}: within a precisely delimited class
of finite-state additive ranking templates, no template certifies
strict descent of the accelerated Collatz map. Our contributions are
four.

\emph{(1) An exact transducer semantics}
(Section~\ref{sec:transducer}). A binary carry transducer implements
$x \mapsto 3x + 1$ digit by digit, and its output word factors exactly
at the acceleration boundary; the accelerated map and its $2$-adic
valuation are recovered as word operations on the transducer's output
and input, respectively.

\emph{(2) A flow-geometric reading of occupation counts}
(Section~\ref{sec:flow}). The occupation vector of a run is a path
flow on a finite graph, and one Kirchhoff conservation law,
instantiated state by state, generates the local counting relations
that earlier bookkeeping treated as separate lemmas.

\emph{(3) Exact dimension theorems} (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}). The
spans of the difference-feature vectors have dimension exactly $10$
(Level~2, in $18$ coordinates) and exactly $31$ (Level~3 two-mode, in
$96$); both directions of each equality are machine-checked, and the
certificate integers are identified --- at the tools layer --- as
determinants of witness matrices.

\emph{(4) Machine-checked no-go certificates}
(Section~\ref{sec:nogo}). Farkas certificates refute strict descent
for three template families --- Level~2 single-mode, Level~2 two-mode,
and Level~3 two-mode --- each in a finite-witness version and a
universal version over all odd $x > 1$, with an affine strengthening
(unconstrained per-mode intercepts) for the two-mode families.

% Non-claims — fixed by the outline; BOTH sentences mandatory here in
% the introduction, not only in Section 6.
Two non-claims are part of the result and are stated explicitly. This
paper does \emph{not} prove the Collatz conjecture. And it does
\emph{not} establish the impossibility of all finite-state methods:
the theorems delimit specific template classes, and the scope boundary
is made precise in Section~\ref{sec:scoping}.

\paragraph{Terminology.}
% Glossary fixed at skeleton stage (paper-outline.md); definitions locked,
% wording of surrounding prose free in later PRs.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{occupation vector} --- the vector of per-edge visit counts
    accumulated by one run of the transducer over an input word.
  \item \textbf{valuation-parity mode} --- the binary mode
    $m(x)$ read off the occupation feature that records the parity-relevant
    exit of the K region; the mode splits the two-register templates.
  \item \textbf{additive ranking template} --- a potential of the form
    $V(x) = \theta^{\mathsf T} F(x)$ (single mode) or
    $V(x) = \beta_{m(x)} + \theta_{m(x)}^{\mathsf T} F(x)$ (two-mode
    affine), evaluated on occupation features $F$.
  \item \textbf{accelerated Collatz map} --- $T_{\mathrm{odd}}(x) =
    (3x+1)/2^{\nu_2(3x+1)}$ on odd $x$.
  \item \textbf{Farkas certificate} --- a nonnegative rational combination
    $\lambda$ of witness rows whose aggregate annihilates all admissible
    descent directions, refuting strict descent.
  \item \textbf{mode-flow balance} --- the certificate identity
    $\sum_i \lambda_i (e_{m(y_i)} - e_{m(x_i)}) = 0$, which makes the
    per-mode intercepts $\beta_m$ free in the affine strengthening.
  \item \textbf{trimmed product} --- the useful part (reachable and
    co-reachable) of a product of a cost automaton with a language
    acceptor; used only in the discussion of Section~\ref{sec:scoping}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Transducer Semantics}\label{sec:transducer}
% =========================================================================

% Sources (repo-only): Core/Collatz_FST_Statements.lean (outBit, nextCarry,
% run, transduce, Todd; soundness pair), Core/Collatz_FST_Phase.lean
% (Phase K/S, extIn sentinels), Core/Collatz_FST_Ext.lean (BreaksAlt,
% altPrefixLen). Definition-layer description only; every theorem-level
% claim is one of the four \paperthm statements. No convergence claims;
% no base-10 material (that belongs to Section 7).

The arithmetic step $x \mapsto 3x + 1$ is realized exactly by a
\emph{binary carry transducer}. The input word is $\digitsTwo(x)$, the
binary expansion of $x$ processed least-significant bit first; the machine
state is a carry $c$; on input bit $b$ the machine outputs the bit
$(3b + c) \bmod 2$ and moves to the carry $\lfloor (3b + c)/2 \rfloor$.
For input bits $b \in \{0,1\}$ the carry alphabet $\{0,1,2\}$ is closed
under this transition, so the machine is finite-state. The transduction
$\transduce(x)$ runs from initial carry $c_0 = 1$, which absorbs the
$+1$ of the map, and appends the binary expansion of the final carry once
the input is exhausted.

Two further definition-layer conventions are fixed here once and used by
the feature levels of Sections~\ref{sec:flow}--\ref{sec:nogo}. First,
feature-level runs read the \emph{sentinel-extended} input
$\extIn(x) = \digitsTwo(x) \listcat 00$, the input word followed by two
sentinel zeros; their effect --- every run ends in a flushed terminal
configuration --- is stated precisely by the terminal-state theorems of
Section~\ref{sec:flow}. Second, each feature-level state carries a
\emph{phase} $P \in \{K, S\}$: phase $K$ is kept while the machine
outputs $0$s, flips to $S$ at the first output bit $1$, and $S$ is
absorbing --- the $K$ region thus marks the output prefix that
acceleration truncates, and the valuation-parity mode of the two-register
templates (Section~\ref{sec:intro}) is read off the exit from the $K$
region.

The transducer computes the map exactly, with no side condition on $x$:

\begin{paperthm}{transducer-soundness}\end{paperthm}

Moreover, the output word splits exactly at the acceleration boundary:
its maximal zero prefix has length exactly $\vtwo(3x+1)$, and the split
is an identity of words, so truncation acts locally on the output and
does not interfere with the higher-order bits. Here $\zeros{n}$ denotes
the word of $n$ zeros and $\dropZeros(w)$ the word $w$ with its maximal
zero prefix removed.

\begin{paperthm}{transducer-split}\end{paperthm}

Acceleration thus becomes a word operation: strip the maximal zero
prefix, then re-read the remainder as a number. This recovers the
accelerated Collatz map $\Todd$ of Section~\ref{sec:intro}:

\begin{paperthm}{todd-eq-dropwhile}\end{paperthm}

Finally, the acceleration exponent is \emph{syntactic}: it can be read
off the input word without running the machine. Let $\altPrefixLen(x)$
be the length of the maximal prefix of $\digitsTwo(x)$ that agrees with
the alternating pattern $1,0,1,0,\dots$ --- formally, the least $i$ such
that bit $i$ of $x$ differs from the pattern bit $[i \text{ even}]$.

\begin{paperthm}{valuation-alt-prefix}\end{paperthm}

These four statements are the entire semantic interface between the
arithmetic map and the automaton layers: from
Section~\ref{sec:flow} onward, every quantity in this paper is a
function of finite runs of a finite machine over $\extIn(x)$.

\section{Flow Conservation}\label{sec:flow}
% =========================================================================

% Sources (repo-only): Core/Collatz_FST_Level2.lean (step2/run2/
% microTrace2/occ2, S8), ProjectA/Collatz_FST_Flow.lean (conservation
% chain, inEdges, terminal states), ProjectA/Collatz_FST_FlowDelta.lean
% (difference layer, (2,K,0) row), ProjectA/Collatz_FST_L3_Flow.lean and
% _L3_2Mode_Recon.lean (Level 3 state and terminals), ROADMAP-A A-4
% (structural-identification narrative). Row-space membership claims
% carry the tools anchor (a3_functionals.py). Rule in force: "absorbed"
% must NOT read as "unimportant" -- it is structural placement.

A run of the machine is a path in a finite graph, and every count used
in this paper is a count along that path. The Level~2 state refines the
carry-and-phase state of Section~\ref{sec:transducer} with the previous
output bit: a state is a triple $(c, P, p)$, and a transition edge is a
pair (state, input bit). For a start state $s$ and an input word $w$,
the trace $\microTraceTwo(s, w)$ records the sequence of steps, and the
\emph{occupation count} $\occtwo(s, w, e)$ of an edge $e$ is the number
of steps of that trace equal to $e$. The occupation vector of
Section~\ref{sec:intro} is exactly this family of per-edge counts,
collected over a fixed enumeration of the edges into the feature vector
$F(x)$ of the canonical run: start state $(1, K, 0)$ --- initial carry
$1$ as in $\transduce$, phase $K$, previous output $0$ --- over the
sentinel-extended input $\extIn(x)$.

Because a run is a path, visits balance at every state: what enters $g$
must leave $g$, up to the two endpoints of the path. This Kirchhoff
conservation law is a single theorem, quantified over all target states
$g$, start states $s$ in the reachable state set $\Seight$, and binary
words $w$. Here $\inEdges(g)$ enumerates the edges $(h, b)$ whose
transition lands in $g$, the two occupation terms on the right-hand side
are the out-edges of $g$, and the Iverson bracket $\iv{\cdot}$ corrects
for the endpoints.

\begin{paperthm}{flow-kirchhoff-l2}\end{paperthm}

The endpoint corrections are pinned down once and for all by the
terminal-state theorems: over the sentinel-extended input the final
carry is flushed and the phase has left $K$, so the terminal state
always lies in a fixed two-element set.

\begin{paperthm}{terminal-l2}\end{paperthm}

The Level~3 machine, used by the two-mode templates of
Sections~\ref{sec:geometry}--\ref{sec:nogo}, refines the state with the
last \emph{two} output bits $H = (d_{i-2}, d_{i-1})$ in place of the
single previous bit; its canonical runs start at $(1, K, 0, 0)$, and the
terminal state again lies in a two-element set --- two, out of the four
possible values of the output-history pair.

\begin{paperthm}{terminal-l3}\end{paperthm}

That the terminal state is pinned to a \emph{two-element} set rather
than to a single state is load-bearing: in the difference-layer account
of Section~\ref{sec:geometry}, the terminal corrections of the two
possible endpoints do not cancel state by state, and the two terminal
conservation rows enter that account merged into one.

Passing to differences makes the conservation law template-ready. Write
$\dF(x)$ for the difference feature vector $F(\Todd(x)) - F(x)$, and
$\dF(x)_i$ for its $i$-th coordinate ($0$-based, in the fixed edge
enumeration). Instantiating the conservation law at a state $g$ for the
runs of $x$ and of $\Todd(x)$ and subtracting, the initial corrections
agree and cancel, leaving a linear identity among the coordinates of
$\dF(x)$. At the state $(2, K, 0)$ this identity reads:

\begin{paperthm}{flow-2k0-alternating}\end{paperthm}

This is the first structural identification: the relation above is
precisely the K-region alternating count --- the counting constraint on
the alternating passage through the $K$ region --- which earlier
bookkeeping treated as an independent combinatorial lemma requiring its
own proof. It is not independent: it is the Kirchhoff row of the named
state $(2, K, 0)$. The conclusion is a positive provenance, not a
redundancy remark.

The second structural identification concerns the boundary step, the
unique exit from $K$ to $S$.

\begin{paperthm}{boundary-step-unique}\end{paperthm}

Exactly one step starts in phase $K$ and outputs $1$: the step that
triggers survival. At the feature layer this uniqueness does not need to
be imposed as a further constraint --- the linear relation it induces
lies in the row space spanned by the dead-state and conservation
functionals (exact rational rank verification:
\texttt{tools/a3\_functionals.py}, run in CI on every push). We state
the upgrade deliberately in positive form: boundary uniqueness, like
the alternating count, is part of the same conservation structure, and
it is the structural fact that later makes the valuation-parity mode of
the two-register templates well defined
(Sections~\ref{sec:geometry}--\ref{sec:nogo}). What looks like a list
of independent counting lemmas is a single conservation law evaluated
at its states.

\section{Exact Geometry}\label{sec:geometry}
% =========================================================================

% Sources (repo-only): STATUS.md (dimension chains; three-row table),
% ROADMAP-A A-3/A-4 (method outline; Cramer law; det-31 observation),
% Dim/Delta file docstrings (Sol/pick, dF96, 65-functional account),
% L3_2Mode_Recon.lean (48-dim ordering, coordinate 33). Tools anchors:
% a3_functionals.py (L2 completeness rank 8), l3_recon.py incl. (6)
% (L3 recon, mode-splitting reconciliation), gen_l3dim.py (byte
% reproducibility; unimodular inverse, max|B| = 3), certificates.py
% --cramer (Cramer law). Rules in force: Cramer law labeled TOOLS-LAYER,
% not a Lean theorem; 31x31 row-level content stays out of the text
% (artifact only; mechanism + one decide+kernel sentence); the
% "Sigma-lambda is not a determinant" guard is mandatory.

Section~\ref{sec:flow} produced linear identities satisfied by every
difference vector; this section shows that the identities found are all
of them. Write $\dFQ(x)$ for $\dF(x)$ viewed as a vector of rationals,
with one coordinate per Level~2 edge --- $18$ in the fixed enumeration.
For the two-mode Level~3 templates the natural difference object is
\emph{doubled}: $\dFQninetysix(x)$ has two blocks of $48$ coordinates,
one per weight vector, with the features of the target $\Todd(x)$ added
to the block selected by the mode of $\Todd(x)$ and the features of the
source $x$ subtracted from the block selected by the mode of $x$ --- so
that a two-mode descent difference is read off blockwise. The spans of
these difference vectors have exact dimension:

\begin{paperthm}{dim-l2-eq-10}\end{paperthm}

\begin{paperthm}{dim-l3-eq-31}\end{paperthm}

Both upper bounds follow one pattern. The difference-layer functionals
of Section~\ref{sec:flow} --- dead coordinates and conservation rows,
with the two terminal rows merged --- cut out a solution space
containing every difference vector, and projection to a set of free
coordinates ($10$ at Level~2, $31$ at Level~3) is injective on that
solution space: the remaining coordinates are linear in the free ones.
Hence the dimensions are at most $10$ and $31$. At Level~2 the family is
complete in exact arithmetic: two dead coordinates plus seven usable
conservation rows have rank $8 = 18 - 10$
(\texttt{tools/a3\_functionals.py}, exact rational, CI-enforced). At
Level~3 the account comprises $65$ functionals --- $40$ lifted dead
coordinates, $22$ lifted clean-flow rows, $2$ block-dependent dead
coordinates, and one cross-block functional discussed below
(\texttt{tools/l3\_recon.py}); the two Level-3 dimension files are
machine-generated, and CI re-runs the generator and requires a
byte-for-byte match (\texttt{tools/gen\_l3dim.py}).

The lower bounds are witness computations. At Level~2 the ten Farkas
witnesses of Section~\ref{sec:nogo} already suffice: projected to the
ten free coordinates they form a $10 \times 10$ integer matrix with
determinant $31 \neq 0$ (cross-checked in exact arithmetic by
\texttt{tools/a3\_functionals.py}). At Level~3, $31$ witnesses project
to a $31 \times 31$ matrix that is \emph{unimodular} --- determinant
$1$ --- so its inverse $B$ is an integer matrix with entries of absolute
value at most $3$, and the independence certificate is an explicit
integer combination (re-verified in pure integers at generation time by
\texttt{tools/gen\_l3dim.py}). In the formal development every witness
row is a literal integer vector: after a simp-normalization each row
identity closes by kernel-checked \texttt{decide}, and the row-level
content lives in the generated files of the artifact
(Section~\ref{sec:artifact}), not in this text.

A computational law connects these determinants with the certificate
integers of Section~\ref{sec:nogo}. It is a \emph{tools-layer} result,
verified in exact rational arithmetic by
\texttt{tools/certificates.py --cramer} on every CI push; it is not a
Lean theorem. For each of the three certificates, the witness rows
annihilated by the Farkas combination $\lambda$ have rank $m - 1$ ($m$
the number of witnesses), so $\lambda$ is unique up to scale; taking a
full-rank $(m-1)$-row submatrix of the annihilated stack, $\lambda$ is
its vector of maximal minors divided by their gcd (Cramer's cofactor
vector), and every nonzero coordinate of the aggregate is $\pm$ a
maximal minor over the same gcd. In the single-mode case this
degenerates to the cleanest form: $\lambda$ is a row of the adjugate of
the free-coordinate witness matrix, and the certificate scale $31$
\emph{is} its determinant --- the same $31$ as the lower-bound
determinant above. The certificate solves in integers at scale $31$
because that scale is a determinant, not a numerical accident. One
mis-inference must be blocked: $\Sigma\lambda$ --- $1024$, $7826$,
$31746$ for the three certificates --- is \emph{not} any of these
determinants; the single-mode certificate itself is the counterexample
($\Sigma\lambda = 1024$ while the determinant is $31$), and
\texttt{tools/certificates.py} guards this inference in CI.

Why is the two-mode dimension $31$ and not twice the single-mode story?
Mode splitting changes which relations survive, and the change is
carried by exactly one functional. The source identity is the exit
uniqueness of Section~\ref{sec:flow} lifted to Level~3: coordinates
$16$ and $33$ of the $48$-dimensional enumeration index the two exit
edges of the $K$ region, and exactly one of the two exits is taken on
any run (\leanref{mode-bit-sum-l3}). Whether this identity survives as
a linear relation depends on how it meets the mode split; writing
$\theta_b[k]$ for the functional that reads coordinate $k$ of block $b$
of $\dFQninetysix$, the three cases are
(\texttt{tools/l3\_recon.py} item (6), exact rational reconciliation):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.34\textwidth}p{0.58\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Setting & Status of the $[16]{+}[33]$ functional \\
\midrule
single mode ($48$-dim) & holds, but \emph{absorbed}: the dead and flow
family ($20 + 13$ rows) already spans it; rank $32$, complete \\
two-mode, same block ($\theta_b[16] + \theta_b[33]$) & \emph{not even a
relation}: residue $\pm 1$ whenever the mode flips \\
two-mode, cross block ($\theta_0[16] + \theta_1[33]$) & holds, and is
the \emph{unique} gap --- the $65$th functional \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The unique missing relation is genuinely cross-block: no single-block
functional expresses it. In the formal development the $65$th
functional lands in an equivalent reorganized form --- the initial-state
flow row summed over both blocks, in which the endpoint constants
cancel, so no case analysis on the mode is needed
(\leanref{fstart-l3-crossblock}). ``Absorbed'' in the first row is
again structural placement, not unimportance: it says the single-mode
family is complete without the identity, while the two-mode family is
complete only with its cross-block form --- which is precisely why the
two-mode dimension is $31 = 96 - 65$.

% =========================================================================

The no-go theorems refute strict descent for three template families:
the Level~2 single-mode linear templates and the Level~2 and Level~3
two-mode templates. Each family comes in a finite-witness version ---
descent fails already on an explicit finite set of odd starting points
--- and a universal version over all odd $x > 1$; the two-mode families
additionally come in an affine version with per-mode intercepts. The
witness sets $\Wten$, $\Wtwelve$, $\Wtwenty$ have $10$, $12$ and $20$
elements, and the refuting Farkas combinations $\lambda$
(Section~\ref{sec:intro}) have $\Sigma\lambda = 1024$, $7826$ and
$31746$ respectively; the three certificates are re-solved from the
witness sets in exact rational arithmetic on every CI push
(\texttt{tools/certificates.py}), and the full $\lambda$ vectors and
witness lists live in the artifact (Section~\ref{sec:artifact}), not in
this text.

The affine versions are a genuine strengthening --- the intercepts
$\beta_0, \beta_1$ carry no sign constraint --- yet they are refuted by
the \emph{same} $\lambda$ as the linear versions. The reason is the
mode-flow balance identity of Section~\ref{sec:intro},
$\sum_i \lambda_i (e_{m(y_i)} - e_{m(x_i)}) = 0$, which both two-mode
certificates satisfy: in the Farkas aggregate the $\beta$ contributions
enter weighted by exactly these mode-indicator differences, so they
cancel identically, and the linear certificate closes the affine case
unchanged (ROADMAP item A-2; the balance is re-verified as part of
\texttt{tools/certificates.py} in CI).

Each certificate is the product of a three-stage pipeline, and only the
last stage carries trust. First, a \emph{search} stage locates
candidates --- witness sets and approximate Farkas multipliers --- by
floating-point linear programming; nothing from this stage enters the
formal development. Second, an \emph{exact reconstruction} stage
re-derives the multipliers from the witness set alone, in exact
rational arithmetic with no floating point
(\texttt{tools/certificates.py}); for the single-mode certificate the
multiplier family on $\Wten$ is unique up to scale, so the
reconstruction has no freedom left. Third, the \emph{formal} stage
closes the argument in Lean: the witness feature vectors are not
imported data but are computed inside the proof by the already-verified
machine itself --- kernel-evaluated \texttt{decide} on
$\occtwo$/$\Todd$, with the Level~3 rows closing by kernel-checked
\texttt{decide} after a simp normalization, as in
Section~\ref{sec:geometry} --- and the Farkas aggregate collapses to a
componentwise nonnegative, nonzero combination (at Level~2 single mode,
a positive multiple of one feature weight at the determinant scale $31$
of Section~\ref{sec:geometry}), an identity closed by \texttt{ring};
strict descent on the witnesses then yields the contradiction. The
theorems of this section depend on the third stage only; the first two
are heuristics and, respectively, a reproducibility anchor.

The numeric layer is enforced, not merely claimed. Three anchor
scripts recompute every hard-coded certificate, functional, and
reconnaissance datum of this paper on every CI push: \texttt{tools/certificates.py} (Python--Lean cross-validation of
the $\dF$ extraction; exact re-derivation of the three $\lambda$ from
their witness sets; mode-flow balance $= 0$; the Cramer law and the
$\Sigma\lambda$-guard of Section~\ref{sec:geometry}),
\texttt{tools/a3\_functionals.py} (completeness of the Level~2
functional family and the free-coordinate reconstruction identities),
and \texttt{tools/l3\_recon.py} (the full Level~3 reconnaissance
account behind Section~\ref{sec:geometry}). In addition, the
machine-generated Lean dimension files and the paper's own theorem
index must \emph{regenerate byte-for-byte}: CI re-runs the generators
(\texttt{tools/gen\_l3dim.py}, \texttt{tools/gen\_paper\_index.py}) and
fails on any diff. Section~\ref{sec:artifact} documents this
enforcement as part of the artifact.

\section{Model Scoping}\label{sec:scoping}
% =========================================================================

% The three classification paragraphs below are fixed VERBATIM (English
% rendering of docs/ROADMAP-B.md §0); surrounding prose may be added
% but the claims must not be weakened or restated. W5 adds the setup
% paragraph (three-layer hierarchy, ROADMAP-B §0) and the gauge
% discussion after them.

``Finite-state method'' is not a single class, and the scope of the
theorems of Section~\ref{sec:nogo} must be stated at the right
granularity. We distinguish three layers: \textbf{Class A} ---
single-accumulator additive rankings; \textbf{Class B} ---
multi-register additive regular functions (copyless cost-register
automata); and \textbf{Class C} --- stronger register or
matrix-weighted models. The placement of the present results is then as
follows.

The single-mode no-go theorems fall in \textbf{Class A} (single-accumulator
additive rankings): $V(w) = \alpha(q_0) + \sum_i \omega(q_i, b_i) +
\beta(q_f)$.

The two-mode no-go theorems are \emph{not} Class A: in the template
$V(x) = \theta_{m(x)}^{\mathsf T} F(x)$, the K-region prefix weights depend
on the not-yet-determined mode, so the template is not any one-accumulator
edge cost. The correct classification is a \textbf{2-register copyless CRA
with final selection}: two accumulators run $\theta_0^{\mathsf T} F$ and
$\theta_1^{\mathsf T} F$ in parallel, and the mode-identifying bit selects
one of them at the final state --- the minimal edge of Class B.

Consequently, even if the universal Class-A conjecture holds, the
conclusion is the impossibility of one-accumulator additive rankings only
--- not of finite-state methods as a whole.

% Gauge discussion (W5). Sources: ROADMAP-B B1 (goal statement, NOT
% asserted as fact) and B1.5 data points with mandatory trust labels;
% rule: no "forthcoming" supporting main-theorem strength.

One hypothesis of the no-go theorems invites a normalization question:
the weights are assumed componentwise nonnegative. A natural
strengthening would assume only that the potential is bounded below,
and recover nonnegativity afterwards as a \emph{gauge choice} --- the
intended normalization is a shortest-path reweighting on the trimmed
product (Section~\ref{sec:intro}) of the cost automaton with the
input-language acceptor, with a structured per-register variant for the
two-mode templates. Establishing this is future work; no theorem in
this paper relies on it. Two data points calibrate the prospect.
First, the existing two-mode certificates are not terminal-flow
balanced: the aggregate
$\sum_i \lambda_i \, (\iv{\mathrm{end}(y_i) = t} -
\iv{\mathrm{end}(x_i) = t})$ equals $\pm 428$ at Level~2
($\Wtwelve$) and $\pm 753$ at Level~3 ($\Wtwenty$)
[exact integer: recomputed by
\texttt{tools/b15\_terminal\_balance.py}] --- so upgrading the affine
intercepts to one per (mode, terminal) pair is not free for the present
$\lambda$. Second, a Farkas probe demanding mode balance and terminal
balance simultaneously is feasible at both levels [floating-point
probe: HiGHS LP over odd $x \in [3, 3999]$]; the exact reconstruction
of such doubly balanced certificates remains open.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
% =========================================================================

% Paragraph (a) — outline material, sharpened against the original
% (arXiv:2105.14697 abs page, v3, fetched 2026-08-04); the corrections
% are flagged in the W5 PR description. Verified components: the
% UNARY-representation rewriting system is a previously studied one and
% the matrix-interpretation impossibility (natural matrix
% interpretations, even with dependency pairs) is proved for THAT
% system; their own equivalence construction uses mixed binary--ternary
% representations; the weakenings are proved automatically with
% natural/arctic matrix interpretations.
Yolcu, Aaronson, and Heule~\cite{YolcuAaronsonHeule2021} study the
Collatz conjecture as termination of string rewriting: they construct a
rewriting system, over mixed binary--ternary representations of the
positive integers, whose termination is equivalent to the Collatz
conjecture; they prove that a previously studied rewriting system
simulating the Collatz function on \emph{unary} representations admits
no termination proof by natural matrix interpretations, even in
conjunction with dependency pairs; and their automated prover, using
natural and arctic matrix interpretations, finds termination proofs of
nontrivial weakenings of the conjecture. The present paper establishes
boundaries for occupation-vector additive templates over a binary carry
transducer. The two settings differ in both certificate type and
representation, but carry the same message: for automated approaches to
Collatz-style termination, the choice of certificate class and
representation imposes substantive expressivity limits. The certificate
classes themselves are incomparable in form --- a matrix interpretation
certifies termination of a rewriting system by a well-founded matrix
algebra, while our Farkas certificates refute additive rankings over a
fixed transducer's occupation features --- so the two boundary results
are complementary rather than overlapping.

That work (arXiv:2506.21728) proposes a finite-state symbolic encoding
together with a block drift; however, in its publicly available version,
the local absorption argument and the pointwise descent claim do not yet
suffice to establish the claimed global convergence; moreover, its
potential involves $\log n$ and blockwise global quantities, and thus does
not belong to the rational finite-state additive class studied in this
paper.

\section{Artifact}\label{sec:artifact}
% =========================================================================

% Sources (repo-only): AGENTS.md (pins, prohibitions), lakefile/manifest
% (toolchain + mathlib rev c66c0c58), Audit.lean (#print axioms trust
% base), guard.yml step list, STATUS Tools table. Quantifier wording per
% the W4b review fix: "certificate, functional, and reconnaissance
% datum" (NOT "number" — structural constants are not recomputed).

The artifact is the repository itself, and its guarantees are enforced
mechanically on every push rather than promised in prose.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Pinned toolchain.} Lean
    \texttt{leanprover/lean4:v4.28.0-rc1} and a pinned mathlib revision
    (\texttt{c66c0c58}); a CI guard forbids unauthorized changes to
    either pin.
  \item \textbf{Proof hygiene.} Zero \texttt{sorry};
    \texttt{native\_decide}, additional axioms, and
    \texttt{@[implemented\_by]} are forbidden. The audited theorems
    depend on exactly the kernel trio \texttt{propext},
    \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}
    (\texttt{\#print axioms}, \texttt{Audit.lean}).
  \item \textbf{Import boundaries.} The partition of the development is
    enforced by \texttt{scripts/check\_boundaries.py} in CI.
  \item \textbf{Numeric anchors.} The three anchor scripts of
    Section~\ref{sec:nogo} recompute every hard-coded certificate,
    functional, and reconnaissance datum on every push, and the
    generated Lean dimension files and the theorem index of this paper
    regenerate byte-for-byte (\texttt{tools/gen\_l3dim.py},
    \texttt{tools/gen\_paper\_index.py}).
  \item \textbf{Statement anti-drift.} Every theorem reference in this
    paper resolves through \texttt{paper/registry.yaml}: each cited
    statement is restated in \texttt{PaperIndex.lean} and
    kernel-checked against the original theorem, so a drifting
    statement fails the build; citation--registry consistency in both
    directions, and the requirement that every stated theorem's text
    come from the registry, are enforced by
    \texttt{tools/check\_paper\_refs.py}. The paper thus documents its
    own anti-drift mechanism as part of the artifact.
\end{itemize}
